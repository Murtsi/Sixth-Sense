# AI Glasses for Everyone, Everyday

Sponsor: **Meta**. Source: the challenge description supplied by the repository owner on 2026-11-13.

## Required outcome

Demonstrate a focused everyday use case where the first-person field of view creates a useful interaction beyond mirroring a phone screen. Deliver a functional prototype and deck, with a clear problem, audience and plausible adoption/pilot path.

Display and audio-first experiences are judged equally, as are web apps and Device Access Toolkit mobile integrations. Choose according to the use case and verified device capabilities.

The prototype must address permissions, errors, performance trade-offs, connectivity and device-state changes. Accessibility, intuitive interaction, appropriate consent and safe data practices belong in the demonstration itself.

## Judging

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---|
| Creativity and use case | 25% |
| Technical execution | 25% |
| Real-world value and feasibility | 25% |
| User experience: input, accessibility and trust | 25% |

## Resources

Shared pool: 10 Meta Ray-Ban Display and 10 RBM Gen 2 devices, with charging/hygiene materials and on-site developer support. These are shared resources, not devices assigned to our team.

Amanda Le is the challenge contact and DAT Android mentor. Web-app mentors: Ming Lin, William La, Amy Lee and Odil Nikadambaev. DAT iOS: Dennis Punjabi.

The prize is a pair of Ray-Ban Meta glasses for each winning team member. Selected teams may be invited to Meta's AI forum on 18 November 2026; an invitation is not guaranteed.

## Verify before implementation

Continuous video vs still-frame access; timestamps and capture latency; camera resolution/frame rate; output routing; supported state events; device/account/SDK access; offline behaviour; display and gesture capabilities; battery and thermal constraints; recording/cloud-processing rules; device borrowing time; submission deadline and deck/demo formats.

The supplied brief does not establish these technical capabilities. Record mentor answers and measurements rather than assuming them.
