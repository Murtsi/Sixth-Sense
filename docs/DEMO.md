# Planned demonstration

No working prototype exists yet. Use a controlled stationary or slow, sighted demonstration with a supervisor and soft props. Do not blindfold judges or ask anyone to rely on the prototype to avoid stairs, traffic or collisions.

1. Explain the everyday need and why an eye-level wearable view helps while hands remain free.
2. Show permission onboarding, accessible controls, adjustable speech and the mute/repeat interaction.
3. Present one selected head-height obstacle using a controlled setup. Show the first-person input, short cue and uncertainty without giving a guaranteed distance or safe-route instruction.
4. Demonstrate repeated-object suppression and prioritisation without overwhelming audio.
5. Deny/revoke camera access, cover the lens and disconnect the device. Demonstrate the appropriate unavailable/degraded state and an accessible recovery action. Do not claim an event is detectable until the SDK supports it or the measured watchdog can detect it.
6. Disconnect internet and show the verified local behaviour; if it does not work, report that limitation.
7. Show measured latency and event outcomes with the test conditions. Mark unmeasured fields as unmeasured.

If actual glasses access is unavailable, use clearly labelled replay or phone-camera mode. Explain that wearable integration remains incomplete. Record a consented backup demo when devices are available. Do not display private recordings, identifying faces or device/account credentials.

User research should focus on accessible controls, alert comprehension and trust. Sighted simulation is a usability exercise, not evidence that the product is safe or effective for blind users.
